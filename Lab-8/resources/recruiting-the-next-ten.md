# Recruiting the Next Ten

## The concept

Getting users is product work. An experiment with nobody in it measures nothing, and a dashboard fed by four people cannot tell you much. So the next ten users are a deliverable, with owners and days, like any story on your board.

Three ideas carry it. Your field table is a funnel: people stop at each step of being recruited, and you can read where. Your channels are ranked by your own evidence, not by what other startups do. And at this size you do not broadcast a link; you hand the product to one person at a time and stay for the first use.

## Why it matters

In 2011 a photo-sharing app called Color raised 41 million dollars before it had launched. The app showed you photos from people physically near you, so it only worked where other users already were. On launch day almost everyone who opened it saw an empty screen, and closed it. It was shut down the following year. The product was built, funded and announced. Nobody had gone and got the first hundred people into one room.

The founders of Stripe did the opposite, and it became a verb among early startups. When someone said they would try the product, the founders did not send a link. They said "give me your laptop" and set it up on the spot. Their first users were installed by hand.

A link in a group chat gets you opens from people who are curious about you. That is not the same as use by people who have the problem.

## Worked example, step by step

Sakhli, Lab 8, checkpoint 3.

Step one: read the field table as a funnel. Asked 4. Link delivered 4. Private link opened 3. Got a request 3. Answered at least one 2. Two drops, each with a name: one host never opened, one never answered.

Step two: fix the drop that is the team's fault. Oni-3 never opened because the relative who was to set her up left town. NK will do it in person on Thursday. That is recruitment too.

Step three: rank channels by evidence. 14 of 23 link opens came from hosts forwarding the link themselves, so hosts pass things to hosts: ask each active host to name two others. Both hosts who confirmed were set up face to face; the one set up by a relative never opened: go in person. The guesthouse association appears once, in log 05: one message, low cost. Instagram and student groups: nothing in ten logs, two opens. Not this week.

Step four: put a name, a channel, a sender and a day on every row. "We will reach out to more hosts" is not a plan. "NK asks Oni-1 for two names tonight and visits both on Thursday" is.

Step five: write the message. Who sent me, one true fact from last week, one ask with a day and a length. "Hello, I am NK. [Host] in Oni gave me your number. She took three guest bookings last week through a page we set up on her phone. Can I come by on Thursday for ten minutes and set up yours?"

Step six: send it in the lab. A message drafted and not sent recruits nobody.

Step seven: a target with a date. Six new hosts holding a working link before Lab 9.

## Common mistakes and how to spot them

**Broadcasting.** One post to a group. Spot it: your table has a channel and no names.

**Recruiting people who do not have the problem.** Classmates, friends, family. Spot it: nobody in your table matches your ICP file.

**A message about you.** "We are students building an innovative platform." Spot it: count the sentences about the sender against the sentences about the reader.

**A link before a relationship.** Spot it: the first message contains a URL and no ask.

**Choosing channels by fashion.** Spot it: the evidence column is empty, or says "everyone uses it".

**Counting delivery as use.** Six links sent is not six users. Spot it: your funnel stops at "delivered".

**No one owns it.** Spot it: a row without initials and a day.

## Check yourself

1. Where in your field-table funnel did the most people stop, and whose job was that step?
2. Which channel does your own data support best, and what is the count behind it?
3. Read your message aloud. Is there one true fact from last week in it, and one ask with a day?
