## Pattern 1: Hosts fear the double-book more than the commission

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 03, 05, 07

1. Why does this happen? Availability lives in two places that never sync: a paper notebook and Booking.com. (03: "the notebook is by the phone"; 01: found out from the family on the road.)
2. Why two places? Booking.com cannot see stays arranged directly over WhatsApp or by phone. (05: calls the neighbor before answering anyone.)
3. Why take bookings outside Booking.com at all? Direct guests pay no commission and come back. (07: "the commission is normal"; 04: the returning family.)
4. Why does one double-booking cost so much? A refund, a lost guest, and a story that travels in a valley where everyone knows everyone. (07: "giving money back is the worst thing.")

**Root cause, one sentence:** Hosts run two uncoordinated booking channels because direct bookings protect the relationships the business depends on, and the gap between the channels is where the fear lives.

**Which answer is a guess rather than a quote?** Why 4's "story that travels" is inferred from how hosts talked about the valley, not stated by anyone. Added to script v2 as a direct question.

## Pattern 2: English replies arrive too late for foreign guests

**Interviews behind it:** 02, 06

1. Why does this happen? One person in the household can write English, and that person is often away. (02)
2. Why only one person? Hosting is a family job; whoever learned English at school became the reply desk by default. (02, 06)

**Root cause, one sentence:** English reply capacity is tied to one family member's schedule, not to the business.

## Pattern 3: Repeat guests get lost between apps

**Interviews behind it:** 04, 08

1. Why does this happen? Guest history is split across Booking.com, WhatsApp, Instagram, and memory. (08)
2. Why does that matter? Recognizing a returning guest is exactly the personal touch that makes direct bookings worth the risk in pattern 1. (04: "I would give the good room.")

**Root cause, one sentence:** There is no single guest record, so the relationship breaks at the moment it should pay off.

---

**Strongest pattern:** Pattern 1, double-booking fear.

**Why:** Four of eight interviews raised it without being asked (01, 03, 05, 07), it has a named cost (a refund in 07), and its root cause points at something buildable: one calendar that both channels write to.
