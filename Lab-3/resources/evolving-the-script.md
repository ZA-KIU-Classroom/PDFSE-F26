# Evolving the Script

## The concept

Your script v1 was built before you had evidence, designed to get you in the door and avoid the obvious Mom Test violations. Script v2 is built from what actually happened in the room. Three rules govern the rewrite: every surprise that shows up in two or more logs earns a new question, every question that has only ever produced compliments gets cut or rewritten, and once a pattern is saturated (a third interview just confirms what two already said), you stop asking about it and spend that time on whatever is still thin.

The output is not a longer script. It's a different one, pointed at where your actual uncertainty now lives instead of where you guessed it would live a week ago.

## Why it matters

A script that never changes is a sign the interviewer isn't really listening, running through a checklist rather than following the evidence. The whole value of early interviews is that they tell you what to ask next. If you ignore that signal and keep running v1 unchanged through interview 8, you've spent a week's worth of real conversations extracting information you could have gotten from interview 2.

The failure story: a team ran the identical six-question script across all ten of their discovery interviews. By interview 5, they'd learned that "ease of use" wasn't the real concern, pricing transparency was, a detail that came up unprompted twice. Their script had no question about pricing transparency because it wasn't in their original guesses. They kept running the old script anyway, out of habit, and only caught the gap when a classmate reviewing their logs asked why a clearly important theme had almost no direct follow-up questions anywhere in the data. Five interviews' worth of deeper pricing-transparency evidence were simply never collected.

## Worked example, step by step

Starting point: Sakhli's script v1, seven questions, two already flagged and cut in Lab 2 for being leading or hypothetical.

Step one: reread every starred surprise across all logs in one sitting. The double-booking fear is starred in interviews 01, 03, 05, and 07. The repeat-guest miss is starred once, in interview 04.

Step two: for any surprise in two or more logs, write a new question aimed at that specific thing, not the general topic. The double-booking surprise (4 logs) earns: "Walk me through the last time you had two inquiries for the same dates." Notice the question asks about a specific past event, same as every other good interview question, it's just now aimed at a topic the evidence surfaced rather than one guessed in advance.

Step three: for a surprise in only one log so far, like the repeat-guest miss in interview 04, you can still add a question, but treat the answer as provisional until a second interview confirms the pattern. "How do you know if a guest has stayed with you before?" goes into v2 as a genuine probe, not yet a confirmed pattern.

Step four: check each v1 question against the saturation and compliment tests. "What do you use to keep track of who is arriving?" produced the identical answer, "the notebook," in interviews 01 through 03. Saturated. Cut it, and let the sharper repeat-guest question above cover that ground going forward.

Step five: assemble the final script in interview order, keeping the questions that are still earning their place and adding the new ones where they fit the conversation's natural flow.

## Common mistakes and how to spot them

**Adding a question because it "feels important" with no surprise behind it.** If you can't name the specific starred line and interview number that produced a new question, it doesn't belong in v2 yet. Run it as a one-off test in your next interview first.

**Cutting a question too early.** One compliment-only answer isn't saturation. Wait for the pattern across 3 to 4 interviews before retiring a question, a single flat answer might just be one quiet interviewee.

**Keeping a question out of attachment to the script you wrote in Lab 2.** If a v1 question hasn't produced a new specific in your last three uses of it, it's done its job. Holding onto it wastes interview time you could spend on what's still thin.

**Rewriting a cut question into a slightly different compliment-only question.** Changing "Would you use this?" to "Do you think this would help?" doesn't fix a leading question, it relocates the same problem. Go back to a past-behavior framing instead.

## Check yourself

1. For every new question in your v2, can you name the specific surprise and interview number that produced it?
2. For every question you cut, did it actually hit the saturation or compliment-only bar, or did you just get tired of asking it?
3. Does your v2 script still follow the Mom Test rules you learned in Week 2: past behavior, not opinions about the future?
