# Milestone 1 Rubric: Discovery Review

**Worth:** 10 points, team grade. **Due:** end of Week 4. **How to submit:** push the tag `m1-discovery` to your team repo, then fill in the Google form provided over Microsoft Teams. Both, or it is not submitted.

This is the whole rubric. There is nothing hidden. Each row is one file in your repo. Before you tag, go down this list and check every row yourself; if a row says "present" when you read your own file honestly, you have the point.

## The eight artifacts (1 point each)

| # | File in your repo | Present means | You lose the point if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `00-foundation/team-contract.md` | Signed (names typed) by every member. Contains the sentence "No member may answer a question about the product with: that is not my role." Names how you track who did what. | A member is missing, or the sentence is missing, or there is no tracking method. |
| 2 | `00-foundation/icp.md` | One specific person, not a segment. Three lines that cite an interview log by number as evidence for a detail in the profile. | It describes "students" or "small businesses" in general, or it cites no logs. |
| 3 | `01-discovery/interview-logs/` | At least 6 files, real interviews only. Each log has: who (code name and role), context, 3 or more verbatim quotes, and a line on what surprised you. | Fewer than 6 real logs, or any log has paraphrase where quotes should be, or practice interviews are counted. |
| 4 | `01-discovery/synthesis/affinity-map.md` and `01-discovery/synthesis/patterns-analysis.md` | Clusters are named as patterns (a sentence about what the evidence says), not topics (a single word). At least 3 patterns, each citing 2 or more interviews by number. A board screenshot or export is fine for the map, as long as the file exists and links it. | Cluster names are topics ("payments", "communication"), or any pattern rests on one interview. |
| 5 | `01-discovery/problem-hypothesis.md` | Committed before your first real interview (the commit date shows this). Six slots filled. The "we are wrong if" line names something you could see in a single interview log. Your Week 3 verdict (confirmed, weakened, or killed) is written at the bottom with log numbers. | The commit is dated after your first real log, or the "we are wrong if" line is vague ("if nobody has the problem"), or there is no verdict. |
| 6 | `01-discovery/problem-statement.md` | States who has the problem, when it happens, and what it costs them. Every claim cites a log by number. | It reads like a pitch, or it cites nothing. |
| 7 | `01-discovery/competitive-landscape-seed.md` | Lists every workaround and alternative your interviewees actually mentioned (the notebook, the WhatsApp group, the cousin who helps). Each with the log number it came from. | It lists competitors you found by googling instead of what interviewees said. |
| 8 | `DECISIONS.md` | At least 2 lines, in the format from `TEAM-REPO-SETUP.md`: `Wk<N> · <decision> · evidence: <links> · owner: <initials>`. Line 1 is your problem pick (Lab 2). Line 2 is your prediction verdict (Lab 3). Every line links evidence. | Fewer than 2 lines, or a line has no evidence link. |

## The quality band (2 points)

Read your own problem statement and synthesis, then pick the row that honestly describes them.

| Points | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 0 | Template-shaped. The files are filled in, but a different team with a different product could have written them. |
| 1 | The evidence is real, but the synthesis is thin: patterns are named, but the problem statement does not go further than the patterns do. |
| 2 | The problem statement could only have been written by this team about these users. A stranger reading it learns something specific they did not know. |

## What is not graded here

Interview count above 6, length of any file, formatting, design, or how good the idea is. Milestone 1 grades whether you did discovery honestly and wrote down what you found. A team whose original idea died in Week 3 and who wrote that down clearly can score 10.

## Before you tag

1. Open this file and your repo side by side.
2. Go down the eight rows. For each one, open the file and read the "present means" column against it.
3. Fix anything that fails. Commit.
4. Then, and only then: `git tag m1-discovery`, `git push origin m1-discovery`, submit the LMS form.

Individual adjustments: a member's milestone grade may be adjusted on documented non-contribution evidence (commit history, random screen-share demos, the team's own tracking method), and an oral defense may be requested. Your `members/<username>/` folder is your protection: commit your own work as yourself.
