# Problem Statement

**This is the Sakhli team's file.** Theirs lives at `01-discovery/problem-statement.md`; yours goes to the same path. Copy the shape, not the content.

## The statement

1. **Who:** Family guesthouse hosts in Racha with 3 to 10 rooms, who take bookings on Booking.com and directly by WhatsApp and phone at the same time, like Nino in Oni. (01, 03, 05, 07)
2. **When:** July and August evenings, when requests for the same week arrive on two or more channels within an hour and the host must decide from memory and a notebook which room is still free. (01, 03)
3. **Cost:** Two double-bookings in one season for a single host, one family refunded and housed at a neighbor's at the host's expense, and hosts checking their phones at 3 a.m. in peak season. (01, 03, 07)
4. **Today:** A paper notebook by the phone, checked twice a day, and calling the neighbor before answering anyone when the house is nearly full. (03, 05)
5. **Why it fails:** The notebook cannot see Booking.com and Booking.com cannot see the notebook. "The notebook is by the phone. I check it twice a day. Still, twice this season." (03)

## What the evidence ruled out

We walked in believing hosts lose too much to Booking.com's commission. 0 of 8 raised it unprompted; 7 of 8 called it normal when asked. Killed in Week 3: see line 2 of `DECISIONS.md`.

## What is still open

Whether a host will trust a confirmation she taps on her phone more than the notebook she can touch. No log answers this. It is the riskiest assumption in `02-design/prototype-plan.md`.

## Pattern behind it

- Pattern: Hosts fear the double-book more than the commission. (01, 03, 05, 07)
- Root cause: Hosts run two uncoordinated booking channels because direct bookings protect the relationships the business depends on, and the gap between the channels is where the fear lives.

- **The second pattern (English replies, logs 02 and 06) is not here.** It is real, but a problem statement picks one. It stays alive as the runner-up in `DECISIONS.md`.

- **"What is still open" hands off to the prototype.** The statement admits what discovery could not prove, and that admission becomes next week's test.
